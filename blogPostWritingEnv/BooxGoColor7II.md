## Why did I Purchase a Paperlike Tablet?

So why would I spend weeks of my time reaserching producs, spend $250 (at the time of this blog post) on a niche tablet and then invest hours collecting e-books and curating a digital library when I already had a cellphone that was able to read just about any e-book format already? You first need to understand that reading is my primary leisure activity, but physical books are often not an otpion. The style of books I read are serilalized web-novles that are released chapter-by-chapter with a rapid release schedule. These books often do not get published until 2-3 years after they have been written if they get published at all. Web-novels are also published in a very unique way compared to traditional books. Where each book is often hosted on the author or translators website. A reactive environment for this content allows for reactive in-text citations, author notes, and even the odd picture! (The astute reader among you may recognize some of thease features built into this very blog! While I am not planning in becoming a web-novel author, I feel the features therein offer quite alot of opportunities for creative freedoms and help in writing an interesting blog post.) Even if I had the inclination to doso, the technical needs of web-novels mean that they do not lend themselves to being scraped off the web and home-printed. So a digital consumption method is called for! 

So, if I need a tablet of some varietal for the books I read, why opt for a Paperlike tablet? The decision for a paperlike tablet actually involed quite alot of factors and internal deliberation for me. While I primarly read webnovels, I also like to read manga, and play visual novel, and adventure videogames. An adroid tablet with a more coneventional screen would be much more appropriate for these two use-cases. Paperlike displays have ratther severe limitations when concidering both comics and videogames, most felt in their low refresh rate, and limited color pallette. However, in the end I still decided on a paperlike tablet for three primary reasons:

The first, is that I was curious about the current state of paperlike display technilogy. The last paperlike tablet I had used was a second-gen Kindle tablet inhered from my grandmother.  The screen was a small monochrome number, with large bezels. The refresh reate was extremely slow, taking almost a second for a full page-clear, its front-light was monotone and had noticable strips of light along the top and bottom where the LED’s were situatied, and finally, It had amazons *extremly  limited* OS intalled (-_- I wanted to see what a color paperlike tablet was like, how the refesh rates have improved, and just in general see what the current state of this technology is.

The second reason I was drawn to a Paperlike screen is the eye-strain. Conventional displays, be them LCD, or OLED use bight white LEDS as backlight to display the sharp, vibrant colors they are capable of. This light ends up being primarly blue in spectum for a variety of reasons, but a bight screen is qutite similar to looking at the sky during a bight day. Looking at one during night tires out your eyes, and tricks your brain into thinking that it is daytime, harming your circadian rythim. Wile paperlike tablets still use LED’s for illumination they use a frontlight rather than a backlight, and they do not use the same color of LED’s as they do not have to worry that much about the color of the frontlight interfering with the colors displayed on the tablet. This pulls double-duty in reducing eye strain. As the light is not directed straight into your eye, but rather directed at the display then reflected back in a much less agressive manner, and the warmer, softer illumination does not throw your circadian rythim out of balance. While I do plan to use this tablet to play games, it will first and foremost be used to read. So reducing eye strain is very important to me. 

As strange as it may be, the final reason I was so insterested in the paperlike screen was actually the limitations intrinsic to this display technology. I was curious to experience how playing games with a low refresh rate screen, washed or otherwise severely limited color pallette, and noticable load times due to the limited processor speed worked. I wanted to see if the comics a read were sharp and detailed enough, as I felt that if the screen was sharp enought to do them justice, then reading comics on it would be a very nice experience. I wanted to seee if I would read books in a different way if the web-browser was cluncky, making me more limited to digtital books prepared beforehand. 

I have recently been thinking about how technology affects my mental health, and a big part of that is that I worry that I have too-easy access to dopamine sources. Where the vibrant colors, and instant gratification that would be offered by a faster, more tradtional tablet would actually do more harm to me than good. I wanted a tablet that is *less* alluring to my brain, in hopes that having one will improve my relationship with the books I read by reducing potential distractions!

## Physical Aspects of the Device

[Back of the Tablet and Folio Case](image2)

So lets jump into my thoughts on the physical aspects of this device, starting with the screen: I purhcased a 7-inch tablet mostly becasue its the most appropriate size for my use-case. I will not be wirting notes on it, and while a 7-inch screen is too large to put in a pocket, one of the things that contributes to eye-strain is reading too-small text. If I purchased a smaller device I would have to drop the text size in order to avoid having to constantly scroll. I also wanted a screen with beveled corners, and one without thick bezels. Strangely enough, the bezel thickness limit made finding an appealing tablet quite hard. The final criterion for the screen was that its front-light had to have both adjustable brightness, and hue.

The tablet is fully plastic construction, The back has a speckled texture that is quite nice, and grips your hand quite well no matter how sweaty your hands may get. One aspect that surprised me is that you can press on the center of the back and the plastic will depress a bit, so there is some empty space behind the tablet. The plastic chassis and thin form factor makes it very light and comfortable in your hand for long reading sessions. I purchased my tablet directly from the Boox website, so it came bundled with a folio case that attahces to the tablet with magnets. I really lieke the case, its made if faux suede and the magets hold onto the tablet very stronlgy. The tablet has a sesor to automatically turn off when the case is closed, and turn back on when you open it which is a nice touch.

So, onto the IO! Both the power button, and USB-C port are in strange spots on this tablet! The power button is on the bottom right corner placed on the short-end of the device, while the USB port is on the top left corner placed on the long end of the tablet. Both are strange spots. It’s not a problem, I got used to beoth very quickly, and the usb port being on the side, and shoved to the top corner is actually quite ergonomic as you can have it plugged in whought the cable interfering with your hand. But both have interesting placements. It also comes equiped with a sim card style tray where you can put up to 1Tb of expandable storage in the form of a Micro SD card. No need to pay for the expensive Micro SD express cards for the fast transfer speeds, as this tablet’s CPU cant make use of them anyway! Finally, the tablet has a very thick right bezel, both so your palm does not interfere with the tablet, and to make space for the two physical page-turning buttons.

# On how I Use the Tablet

[Picture of the Tablet on the Library Screen](image3)

So, how has using this tablet been? To start, I Reeeealy like the page-turning buttons. Having a physical button to use adds alot to the reading experience of epub files, and avoids one getting an RSI from having to scroll every couple seconds (It sounds riduculus, but its happened to me before!). When you are reading a web-novel from a browser, pressing the page turning buttons instead scrolls down the page. You can configure the distace scrolled on each app if needed as well. 

The Boox tablet has been able to read every e-book format I have been able to throw at it, but due to its size, the tablet does not handle PDF files very well. It has an OCR (1) function built-in, allowing you to extract the text rfom PDF’s and reflow the page, but I generally prefer to convert everythig into .epub files before putting them on the tablet. As we have run into issues with the builtin OCR function, where it occasionaly overlays paragraphs on top of one another.

When It comes to actually reading on the tablet, I have been very satisfied with the default epub reading application. When you first open the app it will take about 5 seconds to read the file and build and index of it, making chapter naviagation and bookmarks work, and putting images in the correct spot. It also has several different customizable theme toggles, where I swap between two: One being a black text on white background, and another with white text on a black background, where I increase the size of the text by about 50% when in the “dark” mode. Reading white text on a black background is actaully really bad for eye-strain, so imcrasing the text size goes a long way to furthur reducing it.

[Picture of tablet in Day and Night Reading Mode](image4)

Overall, I am very happy with the UI of the tablet. I know many people online prefer to use diferent e-book reading apps on this, and some even go sofar as to put a different launcher on the tablets, but there are a few standout features selling the Boox UI, Mainly in its “Bookcase” style home screen which essentially pulles ebok documents directly from the tablets storage, and displays their cover art along with reading status for that book. The user can select a book to read from this menu and it will open in the Books native reading app. The only issue I have had with the tablets UI overall, is lack of app-specific scaling options. Most apps I read from do not have any issues with this, however Patreon especailly has very small text by default, and the only way I have found to increase text size on it was to increse the entire tablets scaling size as well. Honestly, Patreon got rather lucky in that I dont mind the tablets UI being scaled quite largely, and all of the other reading apps I use on the tablet can be scaled down to compensite iwth internal settings.

Manga-format comic books look fantastic on the tablet, and work perfectly as .cbz files. Western comic books are not as nice of an experience due to western comics using larger page formats I really dont like reading korean style comics due to the need to scroll, and them being intended for taller aspect ratio screens

 – Picture of One Piece

I get a week of battery life out of it with normal use (reading-only, not playing games, and the frontligt set rather dim, around 30% of its maximum brightness.) If I  am playing games, or the frontlight is set to bright it will only last about 4-5 hours. I am very satisfied with the front linght, the brightness scaling is granular enough to have an appropriate setting for every situation, and the light color temperature scale is similarly flexible. In the system tray, there are 3 customizable presets for frontlight control. I have two of them set to the same hue with different luminocitys. And the third set to a rather blue color and high luminocity which makes colors on games or web-pages pop really well.

So, How is playing games on the thing? Honestly? Slightly dissapointing for two specific reasons. The first is the refresh-rate and ghosting the tablet suffers from. While in most circumstances the refresh rate is not slow enough to be noticable or a real problem. However in any game that requires a quick-time-event or similar situation with tight timing, they will be closer to an RNG based system than something a user can properly control. There are also some large issues with images, scens, or areas that are very dark. The display just does not have a large enough color pallete to properly render contrast in dark settings. So, some games will have areas, menu’s, or moments where you really cannot see the screen in any way. Especially if there is small text in the area. However, the games that work well on the tablet honestly feel like they were made to be played on a system like this! Text-based games, games with turn-based combat, and visual novels really shine on this device.

Similarly with my FIIO, I am not signed into the tablet with any of my accounts, meaning that I do not need to put any pin-code on the tablet. There is no personal or sensitive information, accounts, or anything else. All it has are book files that I have backed up to other devices. So If it was stolen from my car, the only loss would be the tablet itself.

## Closing Thougts on the Boox Go Color 7 II

Overall, I am quite satisfied with the device! A big part of why I bought one, is that I was hoping doingso would change my patters and behaviours. Before I purchased this tablet, I had not read a traditonal book in over a year, and while I would still freqently read web-novels, doingso on my phone offered too many distractions to me. I really like Boox’s attention to detail when it comes to the main screen of the tablet. A clear focus on the books I have, is exactly what I wanted from this device. And I have found myself reading more often, reading for longer stretches of time, and looking at my Phone less. The battery life is great assuming you turn the backligtht off. I have never gotten a headache while reading on this device. The fact that it has android is a really big plus to me. I do not have to worry about a large corporation bricking the device, I can install any app I want, and read any file I put on the device without issue. It has definite drawbacks, but for the price-point I paid for the device I am more than willing to eat those limitations.

## My Journey for Digital Hygene and Balance

So, what does digital hygiene mean? 

Digital hygiene is admittedly a strange turn of phrase, but has become an incredibly important practice in the modern day, as more and more of our lives require interactions with digital mediums. Be it having 13 different accounts to payment portals, accessing government recources, entertainment, communication, even ones health is becoming inseperable from the digital landscape with the popularization of heartbeat sensors and pedometers being built into watches.

Conventionally, digital Hygiene is rather limited in scope, only covering ones passwords, digital footprint, and the apps you use on any given device. In my opinion digital hygiene as a conept is a bit more wide-reaching. Even going so far as to encompass how one physically interacts with the technology around you.

## My Digital Hygiene Principles:

 - Passwords should be strong, and ***never*** re-used across any account that has personal or payment information of any kind.
 - You need a robust and ergonomic password manager. By Robust I mean it is well-encrypted and you have a secure password to enter the manager, You should have multiple backups of any given password database file, and should have easy-access to it. By ergonomic I mean that the same password database should be accessable on all of your devices, OS agnostic, and should have good ease-of-use features. Quick copy-paste buttons, form autofill, biometric auth, etc. Think about your online passwords like you do with a bike helmet you need a bike helmet, but with all safety eqipment the most important aspect of any peice of gear is that one actually uses the gear in question. You *Need* a password manager, but just like with a bike helmet, if your password manager is uncomforatble, unreliable, or furestrating then one will be lazy and skip it. Instead you will use your brain as a password manager, recycleing the same password, pattern, or system for everything. A password manager should be easy to use, so there are no excuses for not using it.
 - You need to have your finachial accounts in order, and easy access to them. Not knowing your password to the payment portal for your water bill is unacceptable. And you need reliable access to all of the major credit lendors in your area so you can quicly lock your credit in case of identity theft.
 - all of your important documents should be secured in a fireproof locking case, and digitized in case of the worst. Things like Birth certifactes, SSN cards, automotive titles, and other such documents should be secured against fire and theft, and easily accessable and organized if you ever need them. 
 - Be very intentional on what you post online, where you post it. This does not only mean large posts like this blog, but even small interactions like replying to the comments section in a video, or chat is giving large corporations more data on who you are, what you would like to buy, an in the worst case, what and how you think.
 - Dig into your phones notification settings. Phone app makers do not make notifications to help the user, they make them to make more money. Control which notifications your getting, and when. Its your device, and you cant live your life on it. So dont let your phone control your attention with advertisements and distractions.
 - Limit your access to, and use of social media apps that have addictive or toxic patters. Super-short form content, gambleing and chance-based systems, web pages and areas that promote, or feed off negativity or fear, and even online retailers should be largely avoided.
 - Stretch and moove often, at get up at least once ever half-hour or so, and take frequent breaks to look off into the distance to the space around you. Just looking away from your screen can put you in a different frame of mind for new ideas.
 - Dont ignore the normal part of hygiene when considering your digital life. Your fingers are probably gross, and putting your phone down at the table of your local cafe has introduced all kinds of microbes to a very personal device! Wipe your gadgets down with antibacterial, or alcohol cloths often.

## Digital Hygiene also includes the way you use your devices, and how that affects you.

Getting notificatons atctually gives me some very minor heart palpitations, not much of one, but my body has a physical reaction to them. I hate this responce, and if I could get away with it I would prefer to never carry my cellphone on my person.  When I am at work, I generally leave my phone in my backpack, and when at home Its often in another room. One day I plan on getting a “dumb” phone, but I have yet to find one that I am truly happy with.

The boox tablet and my FIIO audio player go a long way to helping the anxiety I have with my phone, as they cannot get any notificatons allowing me to properly focus on the media I am consuming, wind down at night, and generally be more intentional with what I let into my brain.

While woking through my degree at college, the way I use a computer changed, and I slowly became a laptop-only person. At home I have a USB-c cable that attaches the laptop to a KVM switch sending a display to my monitor, and connecting all of the usb devices I use to interact with the computer. I only use my desktop computers when running intensive games like Elden Ring, or difficult to run CAD suites like Solidworks. My desktop computers are similar to the Boox tablet, and Fiio in that I am not logged into them, and they do not contain any accounts or sensitive data. Instead my digital footprint is created from 3 devices: the Framework 13 laptop, an old iPhone SE, and an old 12” iPad Pro. I plan on replacing both IOS devices with alternatives that run Linux, or privacy-focus android later, but that is a long-term project. 

Alongside mooving often and streching frequently, take some time to consider the ergonomics of your workspace and input methods. I am a big proponent of split, ortholinear, and tented keyboards. I firmly beleve that the normal qwerty layout on a staggared keyboard are one of the worst possible designs they could have landed on as far as computer input methods go. We have the technology to moove to something better!!
